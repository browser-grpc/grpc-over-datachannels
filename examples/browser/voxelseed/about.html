About VoxelSeed

An infinite voxel-world generator that runs entirely in your browser.

What it is

VoxelSeed is a procedural terrain instrument. Every world is a pure function of a seed plus a handful of knobs on the dashboard: type a seed (or roll the dice), pick a generator, move the sliders, and the world re-shapes around you, chunk by chunk, forever. An autopilot flies through the world continuously; you can take over free-flight at any moment. There is no server, no download, and no account — the page is the whole application, which makes it a good lunch-break toy: open it, fly for five minutes, close it.

The three generators

Easy produces gentle flatlands: low rolling heights, rare trees, nothing between you and the horizon. Complex stacks multi-octave ridged noise into mountains with exposed stone on steep faces, sandy lowlands, and caves carved by 3D noise. Biomes layers a low-frequency biome map over the complex terrain — desert with cacti, open plains, dense forest, snow caps on the high ground — and fills everything below sea level with lakes.

How it works

Because terrain is a pure function of world coordinates (seeded value noise for height, trilinear 3D noise for caves), the world never needs to be stored. The terrain streams in 16×16 chunks around the camera, nearest first, built within a per-frame time budget so flying never hitches, and chunks behind the camera are dropped. Each chunk renders as one instanced mesh with per-instance colors for solid blocks, plus one for translucent water — a few hundred draw calls at any view distance, with no downloaded assets.

Rendering engines

Two engines sit behind one interface, and the Telemetry panel names the one in use. The default is a hand-rolled WebGPU engine: WGSL pipelines for opaque blocks and translucent water, instanced cube rendering from per-chunk GPU buffers, zero dependencies. Where WebGPU is missing or fails, the page falls back to a three.js WebGL build and re-uploads every chunk; you can force the fallback with ?engine=three in the URL. If neither is available, the page says so instead of sitting black.

Who makes it

VoxelSeed is a LunchGamer project by John Davis. It began as a multiplayer demo for the grpc-over-datachannels project (a browser-hosted gRPC game server); the world generator outgrew the game, and the page became the dashboard it is today. The project blog, with design notes on the generator and the engines, is at c100k.blogspot.com.
