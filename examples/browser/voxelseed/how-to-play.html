Play VoxelSeed About & how it works Dev notes

How to play

Ninety percent of VoxelSeed is watching. The rest is sliders and WASD.

The autopilot

When the page loads, the autopilot is already flying — the badge in the corner of the view says AUTOPILOT. It steers through the terrain, holding the altitude clearance you set. Use Pause autopilot (or the P key) to hover, and resume the same way. Nothing is scored and nothing can crash; the autopilot is a screensaver you can steer.

Taking over: free-fly

Press Free-fly, then click the view to lock the mouse. Look with the mouse (or arrow keys) and move on the keyboard:

Shaping a world

Seed is the world's identity: the same seed and settings always generate the same world, so a seed is how you share one — send a friend the number. Regenerate world rebuilds terrain in place after structural changes. The sliders, left to right in effect: terrain height scales the mountains; sea level raises or drains the lakes; tree density thins or thickens forests; biome size stretches the distance between deserts and snowfields; the caves toggle switches the 3D-noise cave carving on and off.

Performance tips

View distance is the big lever: fewer chunks means a lighter scene and a higher frame rate, at the cost of nearer fog. If flying feels heavy, drop view distance a notch before lowering fly speed. On machines without WebGPU the three.js fallback renders the same worlds — expect somewhat lower frame rates at large view distances. The Telemetry panel (FPS, chunk count, biome under the camera) tells you what the engine is doing at any moment.

Troubleshooting

A yellow banner in the view means WebGPU was unavailable and the fallback took over, or that no engine could start — try a current Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari. If the mouse will not lock, click directly on the 3D view once; some browsers need that gesture before pointer lock engages. Worlds are generated locally, so VoxelSeed keeps working on flaky connections once the page has loaded.
